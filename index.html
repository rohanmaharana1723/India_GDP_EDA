Insight

India's GDP grew from $37 billion in 1960 to $2.83 trillion in 2019 — driven by liberalisation in 1991, the IT boom of the 2000s, and demographic momentum. The 2020 contraction of –7.25% reflects the COVID-19 shock.

Key Observations
01

The 1991 Pivot

Economic liberalisation fundamentally changed India's trajectory. GDP growth accelerated from ~4% average to 6–8%, and reserves surged from near-crisis levels.

02

Trade Gap Widens

Imports consistently exceeded exports as % of GDP. Post-1991, both surged — but the import bill grew faster, driven by energy and capital goods demand.

03

Human Development Leap

Life expectancy rose from 41 to nearly 70 years — a 70% improvement over six decades, driven by public health investments and declining infant mortality.

04

COVID Shock (2020)

The pandemic caused a –7.25% GDP contraction in 2020. Despite this, reserves hit an all-time high of $590B as imports collapsed sharply.
